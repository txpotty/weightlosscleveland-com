    How Wellness & Longevity Care Works in Elyria

    Low Dose Naltrexone

    Naltrexone is FDA-approved at 50 mg for alcohol and opioid use disorders, while low dose naltrexone, usually 1.5 to 4.5 mg at bedtime, is prescribed off-label. It is thought to briefly block opioid receptors and calm inflammatory signaling in the nervous system. Studies are small. Small studies in fibromyalgia and certain autoimmune conditions show promise but are not definitive. It cannot be taken with opioids.

    Naltrexone Gum in Elyria

    Naltrexone gum is a compounded form that delivers a small dose through the lining of the mouth, and some providers use it as a flexible, as-needed option to reduce cravings in specific situations. It is not FDA-approved. Research is limited. Your provider explains how and when to chew it, and how long to keep it in your mouth. Opioid use and liver problems are screened first.

    Antiparasitic Protocol, Ivermectin and Mebendazole

    Ivermectin is FDA-approved to treat certain parasitic worm infections such as strongyloidiasis and onchocerciasis, and it is also used for scabies and head lice. For pinworm, roundworm, whipworm and hookworm, mebendazole is a standard prescription. Travel history matters. The Antiparasitic Protocol is prescribed when symptoms, travel or exposure suggest an infection, with dosing based on the parasite. These are prescribed only for their anti-parasitic uses.

    Anti-Aging and Immune Support Peptides in Elyria

    Peptides are short chains of amino acids that act as signaling molecules, and our Anti-Aging Peptides and Immune Support Peptides are compounded, research-informed therapies that are not FDA-approved for these uses. Evidence ranges from early human studies to mostly laboratory research. Most are injected. Expect a tiny needle under the skin a few times a week. FDA rules on compounded peptides can change availability.

    How a Provider Chooses Between Options

    Chronic pain or inflammation may point toward low dose naltrexone, while a suspected parasitic infection based on symptoms, travel or exposure points toward ivermectin, mebendazole or the Antiparasitic Protocol. Peptides may be discussed for longevity or immune goals when you understand the evidence is still developing. Safety comes first. Labs or in-person care may be suggested before any prescription. Goal decides the starting point.

    Side Effects and Safety in Elyria

    Low dose naltrexone may cause vivid dreams, insomnia or headache in the first weeks, which often ease or improve with morning dosing. Ivermectin can cause dizziness, nausea or itching, and mebendazole may cause stomach pain or diarrhea. Peptides vary. Expect possible redness at the injection site, plus peptide-specific effects your provider explains. Report anything unusual.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Your provider asks about your goal, symptoms, travel, opioid use, alcohol, liver and kidney health, pregnancy and every medication you take before recommending anything. Any opioid use, including tramadol or codeine, rules out naltrexone because it can trigger sudden withdrawal. Pregnancy changes options. Mebendazole and ivermectin are generally avoided in pregnancy unless clearly needed. Liver disease needs a closer look.
