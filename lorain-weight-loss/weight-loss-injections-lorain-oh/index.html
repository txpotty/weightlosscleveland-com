    How Weight Loss Injections Work in Lorain, OH

    How Weight Loss Injections Work in Lorain

    Most modern weight loss shots are GLP-1 medicines. They copy a hormone your gut releases after eating, which slows stomach emptying, signals fullness to the brain and helps regulate blood sugar after a meal. Semaglutide targets one receptor. Tirzepatide adds the GIP receptor, and studies have shown greater average weight loss with it.

    Who Weight Loss Injections Are For

    FDA-approved injections are for adults with a BMI of 30 or higher. A BMI of 27 or above counts too when paired with a condition tied to weight, like type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, sleep apnea or high cholesterol. They work alongside diet and exercise. Pregnancy and some thyroid cancer histories rule them out.

    Injection Dosing Schedule in Lorain

    Every weekly injection starts low. Wegovy starts at 0.25 mg and moves up every four weeks to 0.5, 1, 1.7 and finally 2.4 mg, with some patients holding at 1.7 mg. Zepbound begins at 2.5 mg and can go up by 2.5 mg at a time to a top of 15 mg. Same day each week.

    What to Expect Week by Week

    Appetite often drops in the first week. Weight change during the first month is usually modest because the starting dose is low, and nausea is most common in the day or two after each injection. In the STEP 1 trial, adults on semaglutide 2.4 mg lost about 15 percent of body weight on average over 68 weeks, and SURMOUNT-1 reported about 20 percent with tirzepatide 15 mg over 72 weeks. Results vary.

    Injection Side Effects in Lorain

    Nausea is the classic one. Diarrhea, constipation, vomiting, heartburn and mild redness at the injection site are also common, especially in the days after starting or raising the dose. Eating slowly and skipping greasy food on shot day takes the edge off. Rarer serious risks include pancreatitis, gallbladder problems and dehydration-related kidney strain.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Screening starts with BMI and medical history. Your provider then reviews every medication you take, looking hardest at insulin, sulfonylureas and birth control pills, since tirzepatide can make oral contraceptives less reliable after dose changes. Medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN 2 in your family rules out GLP-1 injections. Pregnancy and breastfeeding are too.

    Brand-Name vs Compounded Injections in Lorain

    Wegovy and Zepbound are FDA-approved for weight management. Ozempic and Mounjaro contain the same active ingredients but are FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes, not weight loss alone. Compounded versions of semaglutide and tirzepatide come from licensed compounding pharmacies and lack FDA approval, which means no FDA review of their safety, effectiveness or manufacturing. Some patients choose them for availability.
